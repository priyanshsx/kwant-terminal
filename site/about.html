Who builds this

Quartermaster is built and maintained by one person. Here is who, and why.

Why Quartermaster

Rigorous risk data on crypto assets is harder to come by than it should be. Price charts are everywhere; the figures that actually describe risk: how far an asset fell from its peak, how violently it moved, how often it did something a normal distribution says it shouldn't, usually require building the pipeline yourself.

Quartermaster does that work and shows it. Pick an asset, pick a timeframe, and see what the risk actually looked like, with every calculation behind the numbers published in full.

How it works

Every analysis runs through the same three stages, in order. The pipeline stops rather than charting data it cannot vouch for.

  1. The OHLCV Downloader pulls daily open, high, low, close and volume data for the selected asset and timeframe, standardiss the columns, and caches the result so repeated queries don't hammer the source.
  2. The Tick Inspector audits that data before any calculation runs. Duplicate timestamps, missing calendar dates, negative volume, and corrupted price ticks such as a low above its own high. If the data fials, the analysis halts and reports exactly which rows or dates are at fault.
  3. The Quant Analyzer computes the risk and return figures on data that passed inspection. Daily and log returns, 20- and 50-day moving averages, rolling annualized volatility scaled for a 365-day market, cumulative return, maximum drawdown, and the shape of the return distribution.
  4. The results render as interactive charts you can zoom, pan and read directly. The full methodology documents every formula, data source, assumption and limitation behind them.

What it is, and what it isn't

Quartermaster is a research tool. It downloads historical market data, refuses to analyse data that fails its integrity checks, and shows you what risk actually looked like over a period you choose — the volatility, the drawdowns, and the fat tails that summary statistics tend to hide.

Quartermaster is not a signal service, a backtester, a portfolio tracker, or a source of advice. It does not tell you what to buy. It does not predict anything. Every figure it shows describes the past.

It also is not a data vendor. Prices come from Yahoo Finance, a free and occasionally imperfect source. The limitations are documented in full, because a tool that hides its weaknesses is not worth trusting.

Built in the open

The methodology behind every number is published so you can check it, and the calculations are plain pandas and NumPy. Nothing proprietary, nothing hidden. If you find an error, please reach out.
